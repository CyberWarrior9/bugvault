### Four adopted refinements (November 18 comparison)

The operator-supplied comparison draft contributes **design ideas**, not verified target evidence or a grant. These four additions are adopted with the existing fail-closed scope, privacy and causality requirements intact; its host-wide key inheritance, supposedly safe random busters, header-only cache confirmation and assertion that every case works through `recon send` are **not** adopted.

| Addition | Adopted boundary |
| --- | --- |
| **Per-behavior/layer cache profile** | Reuse source-qualified observations for one origin × effective cache behavior/policy revision × method/path/representation/auth context × cache layer, not a host-wide keyed-set verdict. An unknown key, TTL or hit signal stays unknown. |
| **Key-transformation case family** | Distinguish omitted/forwarded unkeyed inputs from two different on-wire shapes that collide in a transformed cache key yet resolve differently at the origin. Test both only on a proven disposable partition. |
| **Per-attempt isolation audit** | Record the purpose and independently supported key partition for every proposed attempt; a unique query string is not proof of isolation. Reserve any shared-key confirmation for an expressly authorized, owner-disposable entry with cleanup. |
| **Consequence-specific handoffs** | Separate cached delivery from execution, navigation, private disclosure and availability effects. A raw marker is a causal observation, never a shortcut to XSS, ATO, CPDoS or a severity claim. |

## The two exact hypotheses and priority rule

**Poisoning (integrity/delivery):** A controls *one* input on an in-scope, owner-disposable representation; that input demonstrably changes the **origin-produced** response; the serving shared cache's effective key omits or conflates that input; the changed response is stored after A's completed request; and a separate owned viewer V, who omits the attacker input, receives A's marker on the same effective key. **Unkeyed-input** cases ask whether an origin-used component is omitted from the key; **key-transformation** cases ask whether different literal request shapes become one cache lookup although the origin distinguishes them. Both still need origin divergence, effective-key equivalence, later shared reuse and a clean viewer. A public cache correctly reusing a public asset, a Vary-keyed representation or a header ignored by origin is a negative. Harm beyond a marker—XSS, asset import, redirect, degradation—requires independent proof, not a severity assumption. ([PortSwigger poisoning](https://portswigger.net/web-security/web-cache-poisoning); [key-handling research](https://portswigger.net/research/web-cache-entanglement); [RFC 9111 keys/reuse](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9111.html#section-4))

**Deception (confidentiality):** An origin resolves a static-looking path to a V-authenticated *intended-private* dynamic handler, while an intermediary regards the request/response as cacheable under a key not safely partitioned by V's authority; V's completed visit stores a V-only synthetic marker; a second owned low A or permitted anonymous client, without V's credentials or marker, fetches the **same literal URL** and receives that marker in the raw response through shared reuse. The allowed demonstration is V's own synthetic data; never use a real user's session, invoice or token. Direct unprimed A access belongs to IDOR/authz or application-cache analysis, not established web-cache deception. A private HTTP cache for V, a service worker Cache Storage replay, and ordinary public JS/CSS cached as intended do not meet the shared-cross-user premise. ([PortSwigger deception](https://portswigger.net/web-security/web-cache-deception); [OWASP application cache](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Web_Cache_Security_Cheat_Sheet.html#6-protect-application-level-caches); [browser/SW distinction](https://web.dev/articles/service-worker-caching-and-http-caching))

**Prioritization is safety → isolation → causal observability → impact, not a blanket route or payload list.** First review saved, authorized request/response shapes and owner rules offline. Prefer a disposable public HTML route for a poisoning integrity demonstration, and a V-only profile/note field with nonsecret marker for a deception privacy demonstration. Defer production homepage/JS priming, security/payment/token routes and export generation until separately approved, owned and bounded; a route's theoretical blast radius increases the proof requirement rather than its testing permission. Key, forwarding, routing, cacheability and freshness policies are configuration-dependent. RFC 9111 defines a minimum method+target-URI key and `Vary`/freshness constraints, not a universal provider default or mandatory storage. `no-store`, unqualified `private`, response `no-cache`, request `Authorization`, cookie auth and `Set-Cookie` have distinct meanings. ([RFC 9111 §§2–4](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9111.html#section-2); [OWASP classifications](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Web_Cache_Security_Cheat_Sheet.html#1-classify-responses-before-caching))

A query or path “buster” is **not** isolated merely because its spelling differs. Clean V same effective key versus fresh unprimed K0/K2; GET and isolated namespace still need written scope. |

Static **extension**, **directory** and **exact-filename** cache rules are distinct hypotheses. Path mapping, one justified delimiter, encoding and dot-segment normalization should be separated instead of tried together; `/me/anything.css` succeeding at the origin can also be SPA fallback or a public handler. An origin that ignores a suffix is necessary for one deception pattern, but alone says nothing about edge eligibility or storage. Research on parser disparities dates to 2024 (updated 2026) and cannot fingerprint a future target configuration. ([PortSwigger WCD rule families](https://portswigger.net/web-security/web-cache-deception#web-caches); [Gotta Cache ’Em All](https://portswigger.net/research/gotta-cache-em-all))

### Reusable profile, scoped to behavior and layer — proposed only

A future offline worklist should group comparable observations without making `host → keyed components` a global truth. Identity is **origin + selected cache behavior/path rule + policy revision + method/path/response class + representation and authority context + cache layer/POP where known**; a change in any of these can invalidate a carried observation. An unknown behavior or layer is **not** a wildcard that joins other profiles. Store the source capture, time and basis for every facet:

| Profile facet | Useful evidence; unresolved state |
| --- | --- |
| **Lookup/forwarding** | Separately label each tested input as keyed, forwarded, ignored or **unknown** at each evidenced layer; a CloudFront normalized behavior-selection path is not a proved normalized storage key. |
| **Eligibility/selection** | Response status, effective origin/edge rules, `Cache-Control`, `Surrogate-Control`, `Vary`, cookies/auth context and selected representation; absent configuration or incomplete headers remain unknown. |
| **Observability/freshness** | `Cache-Status`, `Age`, vendor signals, owner entry/edge/shield logs, timing *as a hint*, observed/declared TTL and policy version. A hit indicator or `ETag` alone never proves a body was stored or served; unknown TTL is not zero. |
| **Reuse limit** | Profile statements are source-qualified hypotheses until corroborated by a raw-body, clean second viewer and controls for the **exact case**. A neighboring route, auth state, POP or rule revision cannot inherit a confirmed result automatically. |

This profile is a proposed **offline observation index**, not a detector, an owner configuration snapshot or a new probe executor. ([RFC 9211 per-cache signals](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9211.html#section-2); [CloudFront policies](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonCloudFront/latest/DeveloperGuide/understanding-how-origin-request-policies-and-cache-policies-work-together.html); [Fastly key customization](https://www.fastly.com/documentation/guides/full-site-delivery/caching/manipulating-the-cache-key/))

## Bounded, separately classified proof ladders

The ladders are for an **approved operator-run future experiment**, never a default action. First establish an owner-identified disposable namespace; an explicit per-request URL/method allowance; K0 (unprimed), K1 (candidate) and K2 (alternate unprimed) **independently distinct at every relevant layer**; response-class and raw-body capture; expected TTL and targeted cleanup; and two owned accounts A/V, or approved anonymous context. A randomized query parameter is not a key guarantee; uncertain separation blocks a live marker prime. Keep `Accept-Encoding`, `Accept`, language and named `Vary` selectors equivalent except the tested field. Request `Cache-Control: no-cache`/`max-age=0`, a conditional `If-None-Match` or a direct origin request changes the experiment rather than guaranteeing a fresh origin response. ([RFC 9111 selection/validation](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9111.html#section-4); [RFC 9211 statuses](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9211.html#section-2))

**Per-attempt isolation audit (future ledger, not a traffic gate):** before an operator sends, record the intended case, input dimension, **purpose** (`isolated-observation`, `owned-shared-confirmation`, `control/readback`), exact key/partition hypothesis at each relevant layer, evidence of separation from ordinary users and fragment caches, expected owned viewer, rate/budget allocation and cleanup owner. After the send, reconcile actual request bytes, outcome, charged attempts and any unexpected collision. Unknown or contradictory isolation blocks a *live marker prime*; it cannot be repaired by silently changing the buster, tool or POP. An isolated observation may use a buster **only after its keying is independently supported**; a shared-key confirmation is limited to the approved disposable entry, after origin/key baselines, with a bounded count and narrow expiry/purge plan. A hash of a new URL, two `MISS` headers or a database `shared_key=false` flag is not partition proof. ([PortSwigger research on key entanglement](https://portswigger.net/research/web-cache-entanglement); [OWASP complete-path checks](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Web_Cache_Security_Cheat_Sheet.html#7-test-the-complete-cache-path))

| Poisoning rung — A primes, V reads without A input | Evidence to retain; independent defeating control |
| --- | --- |
| **P0: isolation and baseline** | Owner/key rule or layer logs demonstrate K0/K1/K2 separation and K1 cold; known public cache positive control demonstrates the observer can distinguish actual reuse. Unknown internal fragment key ⇒ blocked, not “unique buster.” |
| **P1: origin influence** | On cold K1, A supplies one short inert marker M in one permitted field; origin trace, source-qualified cold response, or independently controlled route shows M in origin-generated raw body/`Location`. Fresh separate input-absent key does not produce it. A marker only in submitted request/DOM or edge-generated block is not origin influence. |
| **KT: key transformation (alternative to omitted input, not to P1)** | On an owned fixture or separately approved disposable entry, compare A's and V's **different on-wire targets**: the nominated cache layer maps them to the same effective lookup while the origin chooses different content/handler. Preserve layer key/config or entry receipts and two distinct origin-route observations. A different spelling, a common cache-behavior match or a cold response difference alone is not key equivalence. A distinct-key control must stay clean. |
| **P2: stored reuse after P1, plus KT when applicable** | After A response completes, V on separate owned client requests the same *effective* key **without M or trigger**; in a transformation case the literal URL may differ but must be individually in scope. Capture V request, raw response marker and body/header hash; corroborate same entry and no V-origin generation with per-layer store/hit and owner logs where available. Keyed/`Vary`/auth variant miss, A self-hit or an in-flight collapsed request is not persistent shared poisoning. |
| **P3: replay and termination** | K0 and K2 clean negative; fixed/keyed/no-store or non-reflecting control fails transfer; only if budget permits, new isolated key and new marker reproduce the cross-viewer effect once. Preserve targeted purge/expiry and readback. Marker at a control key or cleanup uncertainty ⇒ stop. |

| Deception rung — V primes, lower A/anonymous reads | Evidence to retain; independent defeating control |
| --- | --- |
| **D0: privacy/origin** | V's approved canonical read shows V-only synthetic marker. Before K1 fill, low A/anonymous at canonical and K0 denies/omits it. Base versus arbitrary suffix versus one motivated static-looking K1 establishes that the origin **still selects V's private handler**, not a SPA shell or static file. |
| **D1: eligibility/fill** | V's authenticated K1 response contains its marker in raw server bytes; capture effective cache rule, returned `Content-Type`, `Cache-Control`/`Surrogate-Control`, `Vary`, auth/cookie presence and store evidence by layer where possible. A suffix or `MISS` alone does not establish storage. |
| **D2: second viewer** | After V fill completes, low A or anonymous, independently controlled with no V authority/marker, requests **identical literal K1 URL/method and representation selectors**. V's marker appears in A's raw response, with shared-layer reuse and no authorized direct A-origin V read corroborated. An unprimed A canonical/alternate request yielding V's marker instead points to IDOR/application cache. |
| **D3: replay and cleanup** | Unprimed K2, canonical A denial, fixed reject-path/no-store/partitioned-cache negative and ordinary public static positive control. Optional fresh-marker/fresh-key replay only within approval. Remove V's marker and verify **affected cache layers** have expired or been narrowly purged; changing origin `no-store` alone does not clear old entries. |

**Classify evidence one rung at a time.** RFC 9211 `Cache-Status` members report individual caches in origin-to-user order; `hit`, `fwd`, `stored`, `collapsed`, `ttl` and optional `key` have different meanings. `Age` is an estimate across caches; Fastly `X-Cache`, CloudFront cache statuses and Cloudflare `CF-Cache-Status` are vendor-specific. A `HIT`, 200, 302, 304 or identical-looking HTML is never by itself proof that one user's representation persistently crossed to another. Require body-marker bytes, request omission of marker, chronology, distinct negative and adequate attribution; a bounded extra serial read is justified only if needed to distinguish persistence from concurrent collapse/short TTL. If the storing layer remains unresolved, say “controlled cross-context response observed; layer unknown,” not “CDN proven.” ([RFC 9211 §§2–3](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9211.html#section-2); [RFC 9111 Age](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9111.html#section-4.2.3); [Fastly X-Cache](https://www.fastly.com/documentation/reference/http/http-headers/X-Cache/); [Cloudflare statuses](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cache/concepts/cache-responses/))

### Consequence handoffs — optional, independently proven

First classify **cache-caused delivery** using the applicable ladder. Only then assess the following separate effects within the same written authorization; a hypothetical downstream capability is a lead, not a completed chain. Do not prime normal production keys to demonstrate blast radius.

| Handoff | Minimum additional owned-context observation; what is **not** proved |
| --- | --- |
| Reflected HTML/JS → cache-mediated XSS | If independently approved, the clean **owned** viewer receives the cached representation and a harmless browser marker executes in its context; confirm it was the cache-delivered bytes rather than a direct reflected or DOM-only response. A raw `<script>` string, script URL or `HIT` is not execution, session theft or ATO. Hand confirmed execution to `hunt-xss`, with any ATO step gated separately. |
| Cached redirect/link change | Clean owned viewer gets the altered raw `Location` or link from the shared entry; an optional browser navigation must remain on an approved in-scope/owned destination. A 3xx, header reflection or unvisited link alone is not user navigation, an OAuth flaw or takeover. |
| Private response → disclosure | For deception, lower owned A/approved anonymous receives V's **synthetic intended-private marker** after V's fill and was denied on an unprimed control. A real token, payment detail, other user's data or credential use is not needed and is not permitted by this card; downstream ATO requires its own evidence and grant. |
| Cached error → availability/CPDoS candidate | In an owned loopback lab, compare healthy unprimed content with an isolated, attributable cached benign error received by a clean owned viewer; log TTL and recovery. On a campaign, **do not induce 5xx/429, stress service, poison shared production keys or claim denial of service**. Any non-disruptive target check needs explicit operation and impact approval; otherwise leave it lab-only and inconclusive. |

Severity and reportability depend on the demonstrated owned effect, scope and independent validator, not the number of potential public viewers. ([PortSwigger poisoning impact](https://portswigger.net/web-security/web-cache-poisoning); [OWASP cache risks](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Web_Cache_Security_Cheat_Sheet.html#key-risks))

No contrast below authorizes disabling a defense or evading a program restriction. If the prerequisite observation, isolation, cleanup or action grant is unavailable, record `blocked` or `inconclusive` and retain evidence; do not broaden the run, switch clients/POPs or keep trying until a hit. ([OWASP app cache](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Web_Cache_Security_Cheat_Sheet.html#6-protect-application-level-caches)) |
| Tiered cache, several POPs, short TTL, warming, revalidation and collapsed reads | Ordered serial captures after fill completion; all available `Cache-Status` members, node hints and origin/shield logs in same approved path. | Single `Age`, `X-Cache`, 304, fast read or same-path `HIT` does not locate layer or prove persistent entry; do not probe other regions to force a hit. ([RFC 9211](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9211.html#section-2); [CloudFront collapse](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonCloudFront/latest/DeveloperGuide/RequestAndResponseBehaviorCustomOrigin.html#request-custom-traffic-spikes)) |
| WAF/429/5xx, direct origin 200, cached 3xx/4xx | Compare raw body/`Location` and routing source to approved baseline; preserve errors, stop on challenge/rate/degradation. | An edge block, origin 200 bypassing cache or redirect/error cache can be a different case, not the expected private marker effect. No defensive-control bypass. ([RFC 9111 statuses](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9111.html#section-2)) |
| A-to-V IDOR or public sharing | A-first unprimed canonical and alternate key versus V-first primed K1, with explicit intended privacy. | Direct unprimed A access contradicts a *cache-caused* deception claim; hand off to IDOR/authz or intended-public classification. ([OWASP](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Web_Cache_Security_Cheat_Sheet.html#key-risks)) |
| Residual poisoned/private entry | Narrow, owner-approved purge or verified expiry across relevant tiers; cleanup owner/deadline and post-action readback. | Removing/changing V's origin marker or sending `no-store` in a new response does not evict the old object. Unverified residue blocks next prime. ([OWASP purge](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Web_Cache_Security_Cheat_Sheet.html#5-coordinate-origin-and-intermediary-policies)) |

## Proposed offline data and evidence contract — not today's schema

This is a **design contract**, not accepted JSON/config or a command. Store under a campaign-private namespace and retain an append-only event history; an outward/model-facing export is redacted and minimal. Do not put credentials, session cookies, signed query strings or real private bodies into a public report or a cache-key/debug log. Source captures are untrusted data; a marker in them can be a string someone sent, not origin generation. ([BugVault capture privacy limits](../research_notes/Web%20cache%20deception%20poisoning%20plan/bugvault-audit.md); [RFC 9211 key disclosure caveat](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9211.html#section-6))

| Proposed record | Required identity/context and invariants |
| --- | --- |
| **Source** | Campaign, original HAR/Burp/native kind and basis, file SHA-256 or genuine native stored request/response reference, entry selector/import ID, capture UTC time, byte completeness/decoding, source privacy/classification. Imported Burp internal IDs must retain `imported-burp-capture`; never relabel `target-http` or fabricate request/response IDs. Dedupe original bytes **plus account/role/tenant/workflow/source context**, not hash alone. |
| **Behavior/layer profile** | Origin; matched rule/behavior and its evidence or `unknown`; policy revision; method/path/response class; `Vary` and auth/representation context; POP/layer label where evidenced. Each observed keyed/forwarded input, hit signal, eligibility decision and TTL has its own source/time/basis and `unknown` state. A profile is an index of comparable observations, not host-wide proof or automatically inherited coverage. |
| **Operation / request shape** | Scheme, host, explicit port, literal wire path and ordered query including duplicates/encoding, method, body-present/length/hash if relevant, request HTTP version where available, exact tested `location` (`query`, `header`, `cookie`, `path`, `authority`, `method`, `body`), field name and presence. Store selected nonsecret request value only if needed and permitted; otherwise protected hash or `unknown`, not invented reconstruction from sorted query names. |
| **Context and layer hypothesis** | Owned object/representation and intended-publicness source; A and V/anonymous *separate* account, role, tenant, workflow, auth mechanism and session **labels** (not token values); candidate edge/shield/reverse-proxy/application/private-browser layer, key-equivalence hypothesis, static rule hypothesis, policy/config revision and TTL. Unknown identity, owner or layer remains unknown. |
| **Case identity** | `(campaign, class, exact operation/request shape **or ordered pair of shapes for a key collision**, input location+name, variant, owned-object, context pair, behavior, hypothesized layer/key/policy revision)`. A family grouping is only a worklist view. A negative at one path, encoding, query order, Vary selector, auth context, POP/layer or policy revision **never closes siblings**. |
| **Event/facts** | Attempt order/window; source-qualified request and response references; cold/warm and marker placement; raw response status, headers (`Cache-Control`, `Surrogate-Control`, `Vary`, `Content-Type`, `Date`, `Age`, `ETag`, `Expires`, `Via`, all `Cache-Status` members, vendor status) with repeated-occurrence preservation **only where capture permits**; body SHA-256 and bounded marker span; owner origin/edge/shield log reference; separate tri-state facts for origin influence, omitted input versus transformed-key equivalence, origin-route divergence, eligibility, store, serve, cross-context marker and privacy/effect. `candidate`, `observed`, `negative`, `inconclusive`, `blocked`, `stale`, `cleanup-pending` are **event dispositions**, not automatic findings. |
| **Attempt/isolation audit** | Intended case, purpose (`isolated-observation`, `owned-shared-confirmation`, `control/readback`), exact approved key/partition and layer evidence, buster value **only if nonsecret and necessary**, whether its keying was independently shown, expected A/V viewer, allocated/actual attempt count and cleanup obligation. Post-send receipts record what actually happened; an asserted `shared_key=false` or different-looking URL cannot certify isolation. This is a proposed evidence field, not a live traffic permit or shared budget enforcer. |
| **Artifacts/integrity** | Campaign-local restricted path, role (`baseline`, `fill`, `viewer`, `unprimed-control`, `fixed-control`, `replay`, `origin-log`, `layer-log`, `cleanup`), actual byte SHA-256, size, redaction policy and source selector. Verify unchanged source bytes, artifact hashes, labelled context, policy and grant freshness; missing/truncated/encoded bodies cannot prove a raw-marker absence. |
| **Accounting/obligations** | Separate `measured-native` versus `operator-declared Burp/browser/scanner` attempts; include controls, redirects, retries, failed/unknown sends and other concurrent campaign traffic. Declared shared cap, rate/concurrency, window, affected keys/tiers, cleanup owner/deadline/purge authority and independent verification receipt; block additional success recording while cleanup unresolved. Offline ledger checks declarations but cannot reserve or enforce external channel budgets. |

Proposed **case families** (names for specification review only): `P-isolation` and `P-origin` prerequisites; omitted-input `P-query`, `P-header`, `P-cookie`, `P-path-authority`, `P-redirect-error`, `P-method-body`; distinct `K-key-transform` with specific normalization/collision variants; `D-extension`, `D-directory`, `D-filename`, `D-delimiter`, `D-decoding-normalization`, `D-query-GraphQL`; plus common `layer-attribution`, `private-browser`, `unprimed-authz`, `cleanup` and *optional, independently verified* consequence handoffs (XSS, redirect, disclosure, error/availability). Each stores applicable/blocked/not-applicable reasons and the *tested* field/path/encoding/representation/role/layer variants independently. A three-attempt or other finite per-case cap is a **budget/effort limit**, never proof of endpoint-wide or class-wide coverage. Source headers alone may seed a lead but cannot close a case; the existing generic coverage table must show coarse conflicts rather than replace per-variant status. ([PortSwigger two mechanisms](https://portswigger.net/web-security/web-cache-deception); [key-handling research](https://portswigger.net/research/web-cache-entanglement); [BugVault coverage limitations](../research_notes/Web%20cache%20deception%20poisoning%20plan/bugvault-audit.md#2-existing-permission-evidence-coverage-and-finding-gates))

**Promotion is a separate human judgment.** A poisoning candidate needs source-bound origin influence, proved omission **or transformed-key collision** and later A-marker receipt by clean V on a shared key, independent unprimed/fixed negative, controlled replay and owner-context impact; a deception candidate needs V-private origin output at deceptive URL, shared store/serve, marker receipt by lower A without direct authorization, unprimed canonical/alternate denial, replay and cleanup. Store/serve and each consequence handoff are independent facts. The existing `finding move … confirmed` artifact check requires replay plus a *different* negative control but cannot decide their semantics; the [validator](../recon/prompts/validator.md) must also check real preconditions, authorization, impact and reportability. A scanner issue, cache header, synthetic marker or local fixture never self-promotes. ([RFC 9111 security](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9111.html#section-7); [OWASP complete-path testing](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Web_Cache_Security_Cheat_Sheet.html#7-test-the-complete-cache-path))

| **1 — offline capture fidelity** | Optional bounded campaign-local reader for operator-designated raw HAR/Burp request/response pairs plus native stored captures; do not overwrite present value-free import projections. ## Live pilot prerequisites and immutable stop boundary

Before *any* campaign exercise, use [scope-policy](../.claude/skills/scope-policy/SKILL.md) to review the program's published rules, class exclusions and exact host/port/path/encoded variants; put rules provenance in `scope.json.rules` and an operator-reviewed, private `.recon/` run plan. That plan must name approved read/write **methods**, headers/input locations, A/V disposable accounts and object/marker, isolated namespace, permitted CDN/edge host and redirect hops, optional origin/log observer, cache layer/key and TTL assumptions, purge/expiry authority, request/rate/concurrency/time cap across **all** channels, cleanup owner/deadline and stop/resume criteria. Changing a test profile field or creating an export is a separate state-changing action needing method/workflow permission, before/after and cleanup. Account creation, real-secret retrieval, generated exports, CDN invalidation, scanner jobs, deliberately shared production-key priming and host/forwarded-header variations are **not** implied by a GET grant. ([scope policy](../.claude/skills/scope-policy/SKILL.md); [OWASP purge and coordinated policies](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Web_Cache_Security_Cheat_Sheet.html#5-coordinate-origin-and-intermediary-policies))

Interactive authenticated browsing defaults to the **dedicated visible profile through Burp's web proxy** only after operator connection, routing, HTTPS trust and exact HTTP/HTTPS capture are verified; this conversation has **no connected browser control**. An independently approved native request uses `python3 -m recon send` with exact URL check and existing method grants (GET/HEAD by default); it does not provide native Host override or GET-body probes. Burp MCP's direct sends, Repeater/Intruder preparation and settings changes require explicit operator approval and are **not** covered by recon's rate/budget/response-fact enforcement. Its history is not a native request ID, and a ledger's grant can gate recording but cannot meter or authorize Burp/browser/scanner traffic. Manually reserve and reconcile attempt headroom for redirects, controls, retries and unknown sends; pause competing campaign traffic. No missing browser or failed scope/budget check licenses `curl`, CDP, direct origin IP, another profile or a different tool. ([browser workflow](../.claude/skills/scope-policy/references/browser-workflow.md); [Burp boundary](../.claude/skills/scope-policy/references/burp-workflow.md#authorization-and-execution-boundary); [native limits](../recon/README.md#skill-triggers-and-the-request-executor))

**Stop immediately** on possible collision with an ordinary key or internal fragment, unexpected real-user/private material, wrong owned identity, out-of-scope normalized path/redirect, 429/5xx/challenge, lost observer, unknown response effect, spent or unreconciled budget, expired rules snapshot or failed/unknown cleanup. Preserve the receipt, label `blocked`/`cleanup-pending`, notify the responsible operator and resume only after a fresh authorization/isolation/cleanup review; do not purge broadly or evade protective controls. A negative closes only the exact tested variant at that time. ([PortSwigger live-cache contamination warning](https://portswigger.net/web-security/web-cache-poisoning#identify-and-evaluate-unkeyed-inputs); [OWASP complete path](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Web_Cache_Security_Cheat_Sheet.html#7-test-the-complete-cache-path))

**Open decisions before building or piloting:** What policy source expressly permits shared-cache alteration and second-owned-viewer retrieval? Which complete key/normalization/eligibility and effective provider rule revisions can an owner disclose **per behavior and layer**? How can a proposed isolated buster or a transformed-key collision be independently bound to the actual storage lookup? Does a service worker or application fragment cache sit ahead of the observed response? Which cache layers and POP are in scope, and who can verify targeted expiry/purge? Which synthetic private field and owned roles represent the intended boundary? What exact raw-header retention is permitted? Which limited native/browser/Burp action is approved, and who reconciles their shared traffic? Until these have answers, keep the case offline and source-qualified; do not fill unknowns from a CDN banner, research paper, `Age` or default-policy assumption. ([RFC 9211 optional diagnostics](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9211.html#section-2); [Cloudflare keys](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cache/how-to/cache-keys/); [CloudFront policies](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonCloudFront/latest/DeveloperGuide/understanding-how-origin-request-policies-and-cache-policies-work-together.html); [Fastly key](https://www.fastly.com/documentation/guides/full-site-delivery/caching/manipulating-the-cache-key/))
