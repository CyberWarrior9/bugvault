# Preserve business invariants across competing workflows

**Research and implementation plan · as of 2026-11-16 · PLAN ONLY.** BugVault should add two dedicated skills and two offline evidence views over **one shared business-invariant and workflow model**, rather than bolt a parallel sender onto its existing money-path heuristics. The important proof is a forbidden, attributable business effect, not two successful responses or a tool labelled concurrent. The reviewed MindHunt, PentAGI, ARTEX, PayloadsAllTheThings and Turbo Intruder sources contribute useful categories and engineering cautions, but none supplies BugVault's complete policy, evidence and execution contract. PortSwigger Academy/Research provides the strongest combined teaching family reviewed; current OWASP guidance supplies the defensive requirements, particularly durable, scoped idempotency. Implement the offline model and deterministic owned lab first, with the operator-attested lane (M5a) landing alongside M2/M3; the scope-enforced, protocol-aware agent-driven adapter is a **scheduled** follow-on project (§9.2, M5b–M6) whose enforcement design is a hard dependency, not an optional idea. Four operational ideas and two engineering items are adopted from the earlier draft `/private/tmp/plan_business_logic_race.md`: signal-ranked race surfaces, sequence-first escalation, measured-impact fields, control classification, the executor project and the corpus mapping rule (§2.4). A terminal-impact ladder (§6.7), crown-jewel-first chains (§7.4) and an impact rubric (§10.5) then extend the model from "invariant observed" to bounded, owned-resource proof. This plan implements nothing and validates no product's vulnerability or applicability. The 2026-11-16 revision adds three attack-tempo amendments — an anomaly-first hunt log, an operator-attested synchronized lane, and payout-ranked chain plays — while keeping every plan-only and authorization boundary intact.

### 3.2 Native, external and laboratory traffic have different enforcement

`ScopedClient` checks mode, scope, allowed method and pinned egress per request; read redirects are rechecked and write methods do not auto-follow. `RequestBudget` shares target/intel attempts per Recon instance, counts failed attempts and uses a monotonic soft deadline; `Store.reserve_network_attempt()` conditionally increments the persistent campaign counter before opening the connection. ## 4. Domain-owned invariants define what counts as wrong

### 4.1 State, eligibility and value require an explicit contract

A business-logic defect occurs when an actor can violate the application's intended rule while using otherwise permitted functions: obtain an ineligible benefit, spend unsupported value, bypass a prerequisite or repeat a terminal effect. A race is a **schedule/interleaving-dependent mechanism** that can violate the same rule. Authorization instead asks whether the actor can access the function, object, field, tenant or contextual action at all. Record these dimensions separately; a coupon route is not inherently financial and a role matrix alone does not test conservation ([Academy logic](https://portswigger.net/web-security/logic-flaws), [OWASP contextual controls](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Business_Logic_Security_Cheat_Sheet.html#authorization-at-the-business-logic-layer)).

**Policy status has two admissible origins and one explicit absence.** A rule may be `declared` from an owner or authoritative published contract, `inferred-observed` from a controlled behavioral differential on the operator's owned account, or `unknown`. Inferred status is enough to open a T0/T1 hunt observation and to plan the next contrast; it is not enough for `validator-ready`, a reportable finding or a severity claim. The plan therefore separates *discovery* from *adjudication*: find the contradiction first, then obtain the declared rule and full evidence for the winner. Unknown policy remains unresolved for reporting, but it no longer blocks recording the observation that made the case worth pursuing.

For each workflow promoted beyond T0/T1, a domain owner or authoritative published contract must identify legal states, actors, units, preconditions, effects and observation rules. A minimal graph can include draft, eligible, approved/authorized, pending, settled, fulfilled, cancelled, partially refunded, fully refunded and compensated, but **these are examples, not a universal product state machine**. Some systems permit cancellation after fulfillment, post-refund access or delayed entitlement revocation. The documented policy decides whether these states are wrong. Distinguish terminal states from temporary processing substates, and approve exact effective-time and settling behavior before describing a stale view as a defect.

| Domain | Rule to obtain and encode | Effect and misleading shortcut to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Money, credits and gift-card value | Per-currency opening value + authorized inflows − authorized outflows = closing value; matched debit/credit/fee entries; bounded issuable/redeemable supply. Gift-card consume and entitlement credit share one logical intent. | Count authoritative ledger entries and external/sandbox effects as well as balance. A nonnegative balance can hide a lost debit; a repeated receipt can refer to one original settlement. |
| Price, currency, rounding, tax and shipping | Server derives price/product/quantity/discount/tax/shipping from a valid quote/version; bind amount, currency, basket, recipient and authorization. Define currency exponent, conversion basis, fee policy, rounding mode/stage and residual allocation. | Use integer minor units or exact Decimal strings, **never binary float**. Currency exponents differ; not every currency has cents. Client tax exemption/shipping price is not authoritative policy. |
| Quantity, inventory, credits and quota | Permitted integer/fractional units, lower/upper bounds, availability, reservation versus final consumption, per-user/tenant/global/window limits and reset rules. | A displayed stock counter, rate header or successful queue response is not committed allocation. Global and per-user limits need distinct shared-resource keys. |
| Coupons, rewards and referrals | Eligibility, stacking/composition, item scope, maximum discount, use per subject/code/order/campaign, global cap and expiry. State when cancelled orders release eligibility. | Same-key idempotency and one-use business uniqueness across **different** keys are separate assertions. Do not infer unlimited reuse from a finite observation. |
| Refunds and cancellation | Captured/refundable amount after policy-defined fees/prior partial refunds; linked payment, refund and entitlement states; mutually exclusive or compensating finalization/cancellation edges. | Sum refunds against refundable capture, not a UI order total. A compensation does not erase a separately observed irreversible external effect. |
| Subscriptions and trials | Eligibility, one trial per stated subject, renewal/cancel/downgrade effective time, proration, paid interval, entitlement and grace periods. | Event ordering and temporary lag can be intentional. Plan source/lab event fixtures, not real recurring billing. |
| Invites, approval and one-time artifacts | Intended recipient, tenant/role, expiry, current object/version, consume condition, required approver separation and use count. | Object/role substitution belongs to existing IDOR/authz skills. A known test token/session marker does not prove authentication bypass or discovery of an unknown token. |
| Queues, callbacks and tools | Every writer/consumer, authentic event source, intent/event identity, retry/redelivery, acknowledgement, compensation and durable external-effect oracle. | Business-event integrity is not SSRF/OOB receipt. An authenticated webhook can still be duplicated, stale or ineligible; a tool retry can duplicate a side effect even if model execution is fenced. |

Use documented conservation rather than “attacker profit” as the lab assertion. Exact decimal storage alone does not choose a correct rounding/allocation policy ([PostgreSQL numeric types](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/datatype-numeric.html)). Significant transaction data must stay bound through authorization, changes require the documented revalidation, and the final execution gate must enforce the current state ([Transaction Authorization Cheat Sheet](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Transaction_Authorization_Cheat_Sheet.html)).

### 4.2 State transitions include replay and re-entry, not only missing steps

Model skip, reorder, repeat, reverse, edit-after-validation, cancel, resume, finalize and refund as explicit edges. A completed transition must not regrant value; a pending transition needs an explicit ownership/re-entry rule. Re-entry through a retry, second endpoint, callback, admin tool or worker can happen after the original response; it need not be blockchain-style reentrancy or simultaneous browser requests. Follow data from initial handlers through cache, queue, database and external consumers, including versioned APIs, batch operations, GraphQL selectors and WebSocket messages. A state check at only one route leaves sibling writers unaccounted for ([OWASP state machines](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Business_Logic_Security_Cheat_Sheet.html#enforce-workflows-as-explicit-state-machines), [deferred collisions](https://portswigger.net/research/smashing-the-state-machine#deferred)).

API6:2023 covers excessive/automated use of sensitive legitimate business flows, not a synonym for BOLA, function authorization or API6:2019 mass assignment. Business impact and proportionate anti-abuse controls require owner context; a missing CAPTCHA is not automatically a defect and intentional large-volume reproduction is unnecessary for a minimal demonstrated violation ([API6:2023](https://owasp.org/API-Security/editions/2023/en/0xa6-unrestricted-access-to-sensitive-business-flows/)). CWE-841 describes improperly enforced workflow behavior; CWE-362 is broad improper synchronization of shared resources; CWE-367 is specifically check-to-use invalidation. Not every race is TOCTOU and not every bad workflow requires concurrency ([CWE-841](https://cwe.mitre.org/data/definitions/841.html), [CWE-362](https://cwe.mitre.org/data/definitions/362.html), [CWE-367](https://cwe.mitre.org/data/definitions/367.html)).

## 5. Race proof separates transport, interleaving and durable effect

### 5.1 Mechanisms predict different controls and repairs

| Mechanism | Causal model | Needed contrast and repair direction |
|---|---|---|
| Limit overrun / check-then-act | Competing operations validate the same remaining capacity before either consumes it. | Fresh sequential reuse control versus shared-resource interleaving; atomic predicate/update or consumption constraint at the true shared scope. |
| TOCTOU | A checked state/version/identity becomes invalid before the dependent effect. | Identify checked predicate and later use; owner/lab trace if claiming exact gap. Bind check and action atomically or revalidate at effect. |
| Lost update | Read-modify-write overwrites another committed change. | Ledger plus version/history, not final balance only; atomic update, optimistic version conflict handling or suitable transaction. |
| Write skew | Operations modify different records after reading a shared predicate; together they violate an aggregate constraint. | Cross-row authoritative predicate and both transaction effects; row locks on different rows alone are insufficient. Appropriate constraint/predicate protection/serializable retries. |
| Duplicate side effect | Retry/redelivery/re-entry causes the same logical intent to execute twice, often after effect but before acknowledgement/result persistence. | Effect count per durable intent, not request/delivery count; durable scoped idempotency, inbox/outbox, provider-supported intent keys and reconciliation. |
| Cross-endpoint / multi-step collision | Different routes, API versions, tools or workers write the same object through incompatible transitions. | Shared resource and allowed serial outcomes; trace every writer, not just duplicate requests to one URL. |
| Hidden substate / partial construction | A nominal single request exposes temporarily incomplete identity, eligibility or object state. | Instrumented lab pause and protected effect during the gap; safe publication/final execution gate. Ordinary final-state readback alone can miss a transient irreversible effect. |
| Cache, queue and replica mismatch | Checks and effects consult different versions/locations or run later in workers. | Authoritative state, causal event IDs, deadlines and consistency contract; stale display alone is not violation, but later convergence does not undo a proven duplicate external effect. |

“Uses a transaction” is not a sufficient defense statement. For example, PostgreSQL Read Committed permits changing committed snapshots across statements; Repeatable Read still permits serialization anomalies; Serializable requires handling whole-transaction retries. These are datastore-specific semantics, not a fingerprint inferred from black-box HTTP symptoms ([PostgreSQL transaction isolation](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/transaction-iso.html)). Record `mechanism-unconfirmed` when evidence proves a forbidden effect but not a particular internal database anomaly.

### 5.2 Idempotency binds intent, identity and outcome

Within the documented retry window, same principal/tenant + operation + key + same canonical material parameters should produce the contract-defined one logical effect. The same key with changed parameters should follow the specified conflict policy, not silently apply new intent or return unrelated success. Authorization must precede a cached result; knowing another account's key must not disclose or authorize its result. Define resource and API-version scope, pending/failed/unknown states, TTL/late arrivals and which failures are stored. Identical parameters can represent two legitimate distinct intents; a body fingerprint is not a universal replacement for caller intent ([OWASP idempotency revision](https://github.com/OWASP/CheatSheetSeries/commit/0c295f463b8bc5da21b43b9186f3dab8052a0c1f), [AWS retry design](https://aws.amazon.com/builders-library/making-retries-safe-with-idempotent-APIs/)).

Provider contracts vary. Current Stripe documentation distinguishes v1's stored first result after execution begins, including errors, from v2 retry semantics that can complete partially failed work. Its key retention and pre-execution conflict behavior are not universal application rules. Webhooks can duplicate and arrive out of generation order; acknowledgement and signature validity do not prove one business effect ([Stripe idempotent requests](https://docs.stripe.com/api/idempotent_requests), [webhook delivery](https://docs.stripe.com/webhooks#event-delivery-behaviors)). A database transaction cannot atomically include an arbitrary remote shipment, charge or email. Outbox/inbox designs improve durable intent and processing, but still require consumer deduplication, ordering and external-effect reconciliation ([AWS transactional outbox](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/prescriptive-guidance/latest/cloud-design-patterns/transactional-outbox.html)). Neither leases/fencing, request coalescing nor a queue guarantee establishes end-to-end exactly-once effects.

### 5.3 Synchronization is a capability, not an inference from parallel calls

Turbo exposes a shared named `gate` and `engine.openGate(name)`; HTTP/1 last-byte release coordinates completing bytes across sockets, while the documented HTTP/2 single-packet path uses supported Burp/BURP2 batching, one connection and completing fragments. Prewarmed connections can reduce cold-start variation, but warming is traffic and synchronization does not remove server jitter, locks, routing differences or database scheduling. Large arbitrary requests do not magically fit one packet ([gate docs](https://github.com/PortSwigger/turbo-intruder/blob/a6c9a4982fb276167edac0797c7ac747ab9a11da/docs/race-conditions.md), [THREADED implementation](https://github.com/PortSwigger/turbo-intruder/blob/a6c9a4982fb276167edac0797c7ac747ab9a11da/src/ThreadedRequestEngine.kt), [Burp batch boundary](https://github.com/PortSwigger/turbo-intruder/blob/a6c9a4982fb276167edac0797c7ac747ab9a11da/src/BurpRequestEngine.kt)).

Queuing can already send request prefixes, so “gate not opened” does not mean zero traffic. THREADED does not inherit Burp proxy settings; BURP/BURP2 do. Gate-batch retry behavior differs from ordinary sends. Response `req.order` is a transport response/TTFB-derived rank, **not database commit order**; negative timestamps can reflect early responses to incomplete requests, including harmless static behavior. Record actual attempts and reconcile timeout outcomes rather than retrying mutations blindly ([engine differences](https://github.com/PortSwigger/turbo-intruder/blob/a6c9a4982fb276167edac0797c7ac747ab9a11da/docs/engines.md), [batch implementation](https://github.com/PortSwigger/turbo-intruder/blob/a6c9a4982fb276167edac0797c7ac747ab9a11da/src/BurpRequestEngine.kt)).

The separate Turbo [MCP documentation](https://github.com/PortSwigger/turbo-intruder/blob/a6c9a4982fb276167edac0797c7ac747ab9a11da/docs/MCP-SERVER.md) names `start_run`/`start_concurrent_run`, but its pinned [registry](https://github.com/PortSwigger/turbo-intruder/blob/a6c9a4982fb276167edac0797c7ac747ab9a11da/src/mcp/TurboMcpServer.kt) registers `start_run`/`start_run_async`; async return is not a synchronization primitive. That service was not contacted or enabled and is not BugVault's existing MCP. Public [Repeater grouped-send capability](https://portswigger.net/burp/documentation/desktop/tools/repeater/send-group) does not mean this session can invoke it. Browser fetch concurrency also lacks reliable packet, stream, connection, session-lock or server-schedule control. No transport upgrade or unsupported HTTP/3 path is authorized by this plan.

### 6.1 Exact identity prevents cross-object and cross-attempt proof assembly

The coverage unit is **workflow × invariant revision × exact context × case × variant/schedule**, not a parameter field or endpoint family. Use one shared model with business-logic and race views. The proposed `recon/invariants.py` owns typed declarations, canonical identity, deterministic projections and artifact binding; `recon/logic.py` and `recon/race.py` own their case/event views. These files do not currently exist. Avoid a universal policy engine or premature rewrite of existing ledgers.

### 6.2 Typed deterministic predicates are finite, not executable rules

Initial predicate vocabulary: `allowed-transition`, `prerequisite-before-effect`, `at-most-count`, `bounded-quantity`, `conservation-by-unit`, `refund-capacity`, `intent-idempotent-effect`, `binding-equals`, `terminal-state-consistency` and `version-current-at-effect`. Each has a fixed schema and known comparator over named, typed projections; conjunctions are bounded, explicit and validated. A `binding-equals` comparison checks stated transaction data, not automatically account authorization. An unsupported rule stays review-only instead of accepting a free-text expression as executable truth.

Money must use checked integer minor units with declared exponent, or canonical exact Decimal strings with bounded scale, sign and magnitude. Reject floats, NaN/infinity, bool-as-number, overflow, mixed currency addition and implicit unit conversion. Document rounding stage/mode, conversion source/time and fee/tax/shipping allocation. Predicate output is `satisfied`, `violated`, `unknown` or `not-applicable` with typed inputs and source roles; this is a comparison result, not a finding. **No `eval`, imported Python/SQL/JavaScript, templates, shell commands, arbitrary regex execution or callback dispatch** is allowed in declarations or evidence imports. Imported content is data, even when it contains instructions.

Proposed schema-1 limits are engineering defaults, not source-authorized traffic budgets: declaration/event JSON at most 1 MiB; at most 20 operations per declaration, 50 invariants, 100 explicit edges and 32 artifacts per event; artifacts at most 5 MiB each and 25 MiB unique data per import. Reject unknown keys, cycles in required ordering, unsupported schedules and oversized arrays before any writes. Start race schedules with two operations and extend to a bounded **2–8 distinct exact-writer group** when the shared resource and every writer are documented; larger logical groups need a separately reviewed extension. The group size is a precision bound, not a volume target: the 20–30 transport group in §9.2 may carry duplicate attempts for those writers, but it never turns one writer into several. Each fan-out member counts toward the same scenario attempt budget. Enforce campaign-local path containment, symlink-escape rejection and content hashing before transactional event insertion.

### 6.3 Facts separate accepted, committed, effected and compensated

Define the **commit/effect oracle before an exercise**: an independent authoritative ledger/event record, owner/owned-lab transaction trace, persisted consumption/entitlement, or approved sandbox receipt linked to this intent and object. A UI, submitted client amount, echoed marker, `200`, `202`, duplicate response, email or latency anomaly is not automatically authoritative. If the only source is a client-controlled value, record `oracle-untrusted`. If server/worker commit logs are unavailable, do not fabricate them; a reliable authoritative state/effect readback can prove a narrow result without proving internal commit ordering.

Record request prepared/prefix-sent/completed, accepted, queued, worker-started, committed, externally effected, settled and compensated as separate tri-state facts (`yes/no/unknown`). Persist before and after versions plus effect/ledger counts; a flag set once can hide two issued effects. Bind every fact to source receipt, member, owned resource and observation window. “No effect” requires sufficient complete authoritative evidence over the intended assertion; absence of a marker in a response is not a negative control for balance conservation. Do not combine a commit from attempt A, an effect from B and cleanup from C into one successful proof.

Generate a fresh opaque operation marker per operation and independent replay; bind it to workflow/context/resource/intended parameters and a private intent/key digest. A retry of the same logical operation retains the appropriate intent identity while receiving a new **transport-attempt** ID. Same-key tests deliberately share their key within that case, not across independent replay. Controls use distinct owned fixtures/markers but equivalent preconditions. Do not put credentials, raw account identifiers, card data or reusable secret tokens in marker names, dedup keys or public reports.

### 6.4 Time and observation quality remain explicit

Use monotonic offsets for local durations/deadlines, timezone-aware UTC for human chronology, and `clock_id`, source, precision and uncertainty for every timestamp. Client clocks from different processes/hosts cannot be subtracted into server order. Store source timestamps as supplied and normalized display times separately. Authoritative DB/worker commit sequence or causal event IDs establish order **only when available**; request response order, client dispatch order and TTFB do not. Existing UTC-second request rows remain valid historical receipts but insufficient for microsecond overlap claims.

Observation is bounded by the documented consistency/settling contract and an approved maximum deadline. State can be `pending` until then; missing readback becomes inconclusive, never presumed failure or permission for repeated mutation. Readback attempts consume the future live budget. A cache/replica snapshot is labelled as such; compare against authoritative state at a known version before asserting a breach. Later consistency does not erase a proven non-rollback side effect, but temporary display duplication without a protected effect remains non-finding.

### 6.5 Matched controls and independent replay preserve causality

An evidence bundle needs a legitimate baseline, equivalent fresh before-state, exact operation receipts, post-state/effect oracle, a **distinct control testing the same invariant**, an independent fresh-state replay and cleanup. For race attribution add completed A→B and, where order matters, B→A serial outcomes, the concurrent/scheduled trial, and a serialized or fixed implementation contrast. Same-session locking can mask a shared-resource race; separately approved owned sessions are an experimental facet, not an authorization exploit.

If sequential and concurrent trials both violate the rule, the primary result is sequential logic/replay or unresolved causality, not “concurrency required.” If concurrent behavior violates the invariant while fresh equivalent serial controls preserve it, race evidence becomes eligible for human validation; exact internal mechanism remains unconfirmed without stronger traces. Repeated execution must be a bounded predeclared schedule set, not open-ended stress or a success-percentage target. A negative applies only to its rule/context/resource/schedule and observation quality.

Default to **minimized/redacted artifacts prepared before import**; evidence requirements must never require collecting or persisting live secrets or PII. Store necessary sanitized artifact copies immutably with SHA-256, source labels, parent dependencies and policy/invariant/tool/context facet hashes. If an authorized original is already retained, keep it private under access and retention rules; give its redacted derivative a separate hash and redaction manifest, and do not edit original hashed artifacts. `verify` checks all historical events, not only the latest head. Changes reopen only dependent cases: rule, units, subject scope, operation/selector, authority, context, oracle, schedule, tool or precision. Tampering/stale parents withdraw current readiness while retaining evidence history. Cleanup remains an obligation even when a finding is killed or a deadline expires.

### 6.6 Controls, denominators and windows are recorded, not improvised

Every refused or partially refused outcome names the control that refused it, from a fixed vocabulary: `revalidation`, `domain-validation`, `step-gating`, `signature`, `connection-identity`, `atomicity`, `idempotency`, `serialization`, `unknown`. Classification is an observation about which layer answered, not a repair claim; `unknown` is valid and never blocks recording. Two rules follow:

- **A concurrency-time constraint or serialization error under a declared schedule is evidence the window exists.** A database constraint error, row-lock conflict or serialized slow-path response during a declared concurrent trial records the race case as `observed` window-evidence with the constraint named — never as `negative-for-variant`. The interleaving happened and the last line of defense held; that is a lead for a sibling writer or multi-endpoint variant (RC-05/RC-08), not a finding by itself.
- **A race's measured impact is a ratio with a denominator.** Concurrent outcomes record `parallelism`, `attempts` and `successes` together; one without the others is rejected at the schema level. Measurement is bounded and predeclared: at most five independent runs and three parallelism points per scenario, inside the existing request budget; a success-rate target is never the stopping rule. Window narrowing records the parallelism point at which effects return to the contract-allowed count.

Fan-out members belong to one scenario for the five-run/three-point bound: the bound scales with distinct declared schedules, not with the number of writers. These fields make reports triageable — reproducible, quantified, with a threshold the program can fix against — without turning the ledger into a stress harness.

### 6.7 The terminal-impact ladder

A violation that produces no observable gain is a lead, not a finding. Define the terminal effect before testing and record the tier reached:

| Tier | Meaning | Permitted proof | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T0** | invariant observed | response/state artifacts; no durable gain | offline/lab/live |
| **T1** | controlled own-account effect | one extra credit, one illegal transition on an owned object | lab; live C-S only if the program permits state changes |
| **T2** | terminal business effect | one protected field written on a disposable owned object, bounded value delta, entitlement gained | owned lab; live C-S on disposable owned resources |
| **T3** | sensitive read | one sensitive field/record from the **operator's own** test account A or V, server-sourced | owned lab; live C-S on the operator's own account |
| **T4** | chain to another class | ATO/admin/data handoff with exact lineage (§7.4) | owned-lab rehearsal mandatory; live proof per target-class rules |

Rules: T2/T3 proofs are **one object or field per attempt**, bounded, with before/after and cleanup — never a bulk read, a real user, live money or a destructive write. T3 requires `victim-read` plus a redacted derivative and a control proving the server, not the UI, returned the field. T2 requires `owned-write` before/after plus an independent readback. `value-delta` uses integer minor units or exact Decimal and a declared cap. Tiers are recorded, never inferred from a status code; a T2/T3 attempt that cannot show the effect falls back to T0/T1. Where the program's published rules forbid state-changing or sensitive-data tests, T2/T3 remain lab-only even though the model supports them. T4 requires the target class's own evidence and adds no severity by itself.

**Paired owned accounts are allowed for two-principal rules.** Referral/self-invite, invite acceptance, transfer between the operator's accounts, coupon gifting and trial reuse may use contexts A and V, both operator-controlled. The contexts remain separate; V is never a real user. This is the minimum realistic model for business flows whose defect only appears between two principals, and it stays inside the same owned-resource boundary.

**T4 is rehearsed, not assumed.** Gate B must demonstrate at least one entitlement→reserved-action chain, one one-time-artifact→session/account chain on A/V, and one bounded value-overrun→refund/credit chain in the owned lab. Live T4 remains gated by the target class's own rules; the rehearsal proves the chain shape and evidence roles, not target applicability.

### 6.8 The anomaly-first hunt log

Discovery starts from a contradiction, not a contract. A `hunt_observation` records a baseline and a variant request/response pair on one owned context, the field/value class that differed, the suspected rule and a T0/T1 tier. It is deliberately lighter than a case event: no finding, no severity, no claim that the server violated policy. It exists so an operator can notice `quantity=-1`, a stacked coupon, a skipped prerequisite or a stale price before the policy owner has been located.

`policy_status` values are `declared`, `inferred-observed` and `unknown`. `inferred-observed` permits T0/T1 recording, a `recon logic probe` plan and a linked race candidate. It does not permit `validator-ready`, a report or an impact tier above T1. Promotion to a `logic_case_events` case requires the declared rule, the exact operation/context binding and the full evidence roles in §6.5; a dead observation is closed with a reason and retained.

The initial differential sweep is deliberately small and finite:

| Dimension | Baseline | Variant classes | T0/T1 signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quantity/units | One valid value | `0`, `-1`, fractional, upper bound + 1, mixed units | Accepted state or credit differs from the declared unit |
| Price/currency/rounding | Server quote | Client amount/currency change, exponent mismatch, rounding residue | Executed amount differs from the authoritative quote |
| Coupon/composition | Eligible single use | Stacked, repeated, out-of-scope item, expired/other subject | Redemption count or discount exceeds the stated composition |
| Sequence | Legal full path | Skip, reorder, repeat, re-enter pending/terminal | Transition or effect occurs without the prerequisite |
| Idempotency | One intent/key | Same key/same body, same key/changed body, new key/same body | Durable effect count differs from one logical intent |
| Entitlement fields | Current owned value | Role/plan/credit-like field write or replay | Owned readback shows a gain not authorized by the workflow |

Bounds: at most 50 probes per workflow and one owned object/field per probe; no bulk enumeration, no real-user data, no live money. `recon logic probe` only emits the matrix and `executed:false`; it does not send, generate payloads or execute values. A probe is performed through the approved browser/native channel under the campaign plan.

Promotion is one-way: observation → case → finding. A case may cite several observations; an observation cannot become evidence by itself. A repeated observation without a named rule is closed as a lead, not left open indefinitely.

### 7.3 Sequence first: a surviving control is race intelligence

For each mapped invariant, the sequence/value dimension runs **first**: one request, an immediate answer, and a refusal still characterizes the control (recorded per §6.6). An invariant that *survives* a declared sequence contrast with a named control is **auto-promoted to a race candidate** — the surviving control is precisely the check whose atomicity is now the question. Escalation writes a linked race scenario citing the logic event and the surviving control; it never closes the logic case and never fabricates a race observation. Invariants guarding money, entitlement and identity rank first in both dimensions. A sequence violation found first is the logic finding; the paired race scenario stays open only while the same rule remains concurrency-relevant. This rule is why the two skills share one invariant model: the cheap dimension's refusals are the expensive dimension's target list, so no business-logic negative is a dead end. An inferred-policy observation (BL-00) enters this ladder as a T0/T1 hypothesis; once a rule is declared, it can escalate like any declared case, but survival still requires a named control.

### 7.4 Chain escalation is first-class but lineage-gated

A business-logic finding pays when it yields something another class can convert. Add the five hints to the chain table and rank them in `chain_next`:

| Source → target | Chain meaning |
|---|---|
| `business-logic → access-control` | entitlement bypass → action reserved to a role |
| `business-logic → ato` | identity-trust violation → account access |
| `race-conditions → business-logic` | concurrency defeats the invariant the sequence family could not |
| `race-conditions → ato` | concurrent use of a one-time auth artifact (reset token, OTP, invite) |
| `mass-assignment → business-logic` | privileged field set → entitlement granted |

Each hint requires **exact lineage** — a shared invariant, resource or root key linking the two findings — not campaign-wide class coincidence. A hint never creates a finding or completes a template; it only orders the next step. T4 evidence is the demonstrated downstream effect under the target class's own rules, with the attacker-gain line carried through to the report.

**The chain is the unit of work.** Group the hints into three payout-ranked plays: **P1 entitlement → reserved action** (business-logic → access-control), **P2 one-time artifact race → session/account** (race-conditions → ato, RC-21), and **P3 value overrun → refund/credit/transfer** (business-logic/race → value effect). Each play names the terminal asset, the owned A/V contexts, the target-class evidence and the stop condition. `chain_next` orders plays by terminal asset and demonstrated precondition, not by class order. Every T0/T1 event must name its next play step or be closed as a lead; a play never creates a finding.

### 9.2 Future synchronized execution requires a real reservation and transport design

The current native `send` cannot simply gain a `--parallel` flag. A future broker must atomically reserve **aggregate campaign and per-host request attempts, request/response byte ceilings, group size, maximum in-flight concurrency, time window and object/effect allowance**, including preparation/prefixes, warming, controls, readbacks and cleanup headroom. Average rate and instantaneous burst are separate constraints. Policy snapshot/expiry and permitted synchronized envelope must be checked before any member can send a prefix. Budget for one must refuse a two-member group without partial dispatch. Reservation contention must be safe across processes and normal sends; uncertain deliveries remain spent until reconciled, not refunded by assumption.

**M5a operator-attested lane.** This is a planned lane, not current capability. Until the custom broker exists, an operator may run Turbo Intruder or Burp Repeater single-packet/grouped sends against owned, nonfinancial resources under the standing plan. The agent prepares the exact requests, a declared gate/schedule and a bounded fan-out of 2–8 exact writers; the operator fires the gate and supplies installed-version/protocol/gate/send-count attestation. Receipts import as `external-tool` or `imported-burp-capture`; they are never relabelled native, and every declared attempt is charged once. Ambiguous deliveries remain spent until reconciled. This lane is operator-controlled, not agent-driven; agent-driven C-P still requires M6.

Do not keep a database reservation transaction open while waiting on sockets or a lab barrier. Commit the reservation first, dispatch separately, then append per-member receipts and settle unused allowance transactionally. Each experimental operation needs its own target transaction/session identity where required; submitting both inside one target transaction cannot test cross-transaction isolation. Release only proven unused reservations, use durable claim/lease/fencing for result ownership, and reconcile unknown side effects on restart before redispatch. Fencing stale result writes cannot recall an already sent mutation or guarantee exactly-once external action.

A reviewed HTTP/1 last-byte or HTTP/2 single-packet adapter must attest installed engine/version, negotiated protocol, connection/stream grouping, prewarming counts, gate membership, actual prefix/completion release, retries and responses — with concrete defaults from the earlier draft's specification: group size 20–30 bounded by policy (a precision tool, not volume), a transport library that actually exposes frame withholding (an `h2`-style implementation; plain high-level clients do not), per-endpoint delay offsets because endpoints reach their vulnerable sub-state at different times (internal-latency accounting), and the internal-latency diagnostic surfaced in `race status`: inconsistent response times under synchronized release mean backend delay is interfering, which is information rather than failure. It must retain approved scope/egress/proxy/capture constraints at every destination and disallow automatic login macros, adaptive AUTO protocol probes, redirects and mutation retries inside groups. A future external bridge must enforce and attest these checks, not merely accept an arbitrary script or report a declared total after execution. `import-burp` can supply captured content, but does not by itself establish gate timing, attempted-send count or application overlap.

If the stack cannot support approved synchronization without breaking rate/egress/capture policy, **live race execution stays blocked**. Do not bypass host pacing, switch to THREADED/direct HTTP/3, route around a failed check, use request smuggling, add timing pressure through resource exhaustion, or fall back to high-rate probabilistic floods. No current browser or MCP tab API closes that capability gap. Operator-run external activity remains the M5a lane, with exclusive campaign accounting under existing policy; it is not an agent-driven scope-enforced synchronized adapter and cannot be advertised as one.

Stop on scope/method/authorization/budget failure, 429, challenge/CAPTCHA, lockout, 5xx/degradation, unexpected non-owned data/state, unapproved destination, receiver/observer loss, uncertain uncontrolled effect or failed/unresolved cleanup. Sent members cannot be recalled; stop blocks new dispatch and initiates bounded reconciliation, not blind rollback traffic. The offline stop ledger honestly gates readiness/recording until all live channels consult it. Future adapter cancellation, reservation expiry and cleanup obligations need receipts and separate tests.

### 10.5 The impact rubric the validator must apply

A finding may leave `observed` only with an `impact_tier` (§6.7), a one-line attacker gain, preconditions, blast radius, reproduction count and a proposed fix. Minimum reportable impact per family:

| Family | Minimal reportable impact | Required evidence |
|---|---|---|
| money/credits | bounded value delta on an owned resource | ledger before/after + `value-delta`, one unit, declared cap |
| price/rounding | one reproduced price manipulation | stored quote/version vs executed amount, exact units |
| entitlement | one gained feature/role | owned-state readback showing the gain |
| data/identity | one owned sensitive field read | `victim-read` + redacted derivative + UI-only control |
| workflow | durable illegal state | state-machine edge + authoritative readback |
| race | reproduced violation with a denominator | `successes/attempts` at stated `parallelism` + window threshold |
| chain / T4 | demonstrated downstream effect under the target class's own rules | exact lineage + target-class artifact + attacker-gain line |

A T0/T1 result stays a lead: a valid negative or partial outcome, not a reportable impact. A T0/T1 may be linked as a chain precondition but does not become reportable until the terminal effect is demonstrated; chain plays are ranked by terminal asset, and the rank is prioritization, not evidence. The rubric scores tiers and never invents them from status codes; a program policy that forbids the terminal step keeps the case lab-only rather than lowering the bar. Severity follows the demonstrated gain, not the class label or the number of requests.
